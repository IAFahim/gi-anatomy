Fig 3 · how a deposit becomes a cell

The planes

A tile is not a buffer of values. It is four planes and a rule for folding them into one page of short cells — and the folding happens only when the tile publishes.

What the figure shows

  • The I hopper is the int32 plane, 4 096 B. Box, raster and round stamps all write into it, because their contributions are already whole cell values.
  • The T and B hoppers are the int64 tent and bell planes, 8 192 B each. They keep the exact product, so a cell can hold a fraction that is rounded only once, at publish.
  • The trough is the sum. The planes are added together before the result is turned into a short, so cancellation is exact rather than approximate.
  • The page is 1 024 int16 cells in 2 048 B. Only the 472 cells that differ from zero are stored; the rest of the page reads as zero without holding anything.

Plane sizes and the publish rule come from Gi's own TilePlanes, TilePublish and PlaneFold. The cells come from build/model/planes.mjs, which deposits the same kernels Gi does.
