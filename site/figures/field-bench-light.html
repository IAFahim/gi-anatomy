Fig 1 · the change-rate deposit cycle

The field bench

Gi never redraws the world. It redraws the tiles that changed. A source is a column and a mark on a changed list; the press cycle is the only moment the field moves.

What the figure shows

  • The die is a source: a position, a kernel, a gain. Moving it writes a column and marks the slot — it touches no tile.
  • The press is World.Process. It compares each marked source against the 16-byte record of what it last deposited, and where they differ it adds the negated old taps and the new taps.
  • The bright patch is the deposited field: a cone kernel sampled at cell centres, peaking at value · gain = 90.
  • The dial reads how many tiles that press republished. It is 1 while the die stays inside one tile, and 2 when the footprint crosses a tile edge — the old tile is dirtied by the lift as well.

Numbers are produced by the library's own semantics, reimplemented in build/model/field.mjs and asserted against the kernel dumps in Gi's README. The kernel math in build/model/model.mjs is bit-exact against those dumps.
