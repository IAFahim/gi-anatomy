Fig 4 · how channels combine

The balance

Food minus twice the threat. A derived layer is not a special kind of layer: it is an ordinary one whose cells always equal a formula over other layers, so it answers every query they answer.

What the figure shows

  • The left pan carries the food channel: a Dome stamped at cell (9,9), its 109 cells standing at their own value.
  • The right pan carries the threat channel: a Cone stamped at cell (15,15). Its cells stand twice as tall, because Layer.Sum(world, food, 1, threat, -2) counts the threat twice.
  • The beam leans by the ratio of the two loads: 5 058 of food against 6 796 of threat, so 8.3° toward the threat. That lean is the tile's net, −1 738.
  • The page is the derived layer itself: the formula applied to each of the 1 024 cells. 199 of them are not zero - 97 safe, 102 owned by the threat - and the peg stands where the layer holds its maximum of 90.

The recipe, the weights and the saturation come from Gi's own Layer.Sum and LayerRecombine; the cells come from build/model/derive.mjs, which runs the same deposit and the same recombination Gi does.
